# Reference items (validated psychological safety scales)

Retyped from the source papers for use in the overlap analysis only. Spacing was corrected where the PDF text extraction dropped spaces. Check against the papers before quoting in any publication.

## Edmondson (1999), team psychological safety (7 items)

Source: Edmondson, A. (1999). Psychological safety and learning behavior in work teams. Administrative Science Quarterly, 44(2), 350-383. Appendix, "Team psychological safety". 7-point scale from "very inaccurate" to "very accurate".

1. If you make a mistake on this team, it is often held against you. (marked reverse scored in the paper's text)
2. Members of this team are able to bring up problems and tough issues.
3. People on this team sometimes reject others for being different. [Inference] negatively worded, so probably reverse scored
4. It is safe to take a risk on this team.
5. It is difficult to ask other members of this team for help. [Inference] negatively worded, so probably reverse scored
6. No one on this team would deliberately act in a way that undermines my efforts.
7. Working with members of this team, my unique skills and talents are valued and utilized.

## May, Gilson and Harter (2004), psychological safety (3 items)

Source: May, D. R., Gilson, R. L., & Harter, L. M. (2004). The psychological conditions of meaningfulness, safety and availability and the engagement of the human spirit at work. Journal of Occupational and Organizational Psychology, 77, 11-37. Appendix. 5-point scale. Alpha .71 in the original sample. Based on Kahn (1990).

1. I'm not afraid to be myself at work.
2. I am afraid to express my opinions at work. (reverse scored)
3. There is a threatening environment at work. (reverse scored)

## Notes

- Edmondson's scale refers to "this team". May et al.'s scale refers to "work", an individual-level perception. The difference is intentional: the two come from different traditions (Edmondson's team interpersonal risk-taking, Kahn's being yourself at work).
- Detert and Burris (2007) adapted 3 items from Edmondson's scale (one sample item: "It is safe for me to speak up around here."). The remaining items are not published in the paper. It is not used as an independent baseline.
